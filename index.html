Chapter 1 — The warming ocean

The Ocean Is Getting Hotter

Sea surface temperatures above the Great Barrier Reef have been rising for decades — but it is the accelerating frequency and severity of summer heat events that is now overwhelming the reef's ability to survive.
Peak sea surface temperature anomaly by GBR sector (1985–2025)

Annual maximum SST anomaly (°C above historical average) per GBR sector. Vertical lines mark confirmed mass bleaching events. Data: NOAA Coral Reef Watch Regional Virtual Stations.

Coral bleaching is triggered when temperatures exceed the average summer maximum for as little as 8 consecutive weeks. That threshold is now crossed in most years across at least one GBR sector.
Chapter 2 — The bleaching crisis

Bleaching Is No Longer a Rare Event

Mass coral bleaching was first recorded on the Great Barrier Reef in 1998. Since 2016, five more events have followed in rapid succession — with shrinking gaps between them that leave coral no time to recover.

Chapter 3 — Coral cover

What's Left of the Reef?

Hard coral cover — tracked by AIMS since 1992 — is the standard measure of reef health. After a period of recovery, all three regions suffered dramatic declines following the 2024 bleaching event.

The window is closing

The Reef Has Always Recovered.
Can It Again?

Coral needs 10–15 years between disturbances to rebuild. With bleaching now arriving every 2–3 years and ocean temperatures continuing to rise, scientists warn the recovery window may close entirely within this generation.

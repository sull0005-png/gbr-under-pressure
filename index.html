Chapter 1 — The Warming Ocean

The Ocean Is Getting Hotter

Sea surface temperatures above the Great Barrier Reef have been rising for decades. But it is the accelerating frequency of extreme summer heat events that is now overwhelming the reef's ability to recover.

Chapter 3b — Where Damage Is Worst

Inshore Reefs Are Losing the Fight

Reefs close to the coast face a double assault — bleaching from above and land-based pollution from below. Inshore reefs consistently show half the coral cover of their offshore counterparts.
Coral cover by reef position — inshore vs mid-shelf vs outer-shelf (1993–2023)

Inshore reefs (closest to coast) are most affected by agricultural runoff, sediment, and combined heat stress. Outer-shelf reefs have consistently higher cover but are not immune to bleaching. Source: AIMS LTMP.

Live coral vs dead coral — Northern GBR (1993–2023)

Diverging bar chart: live coral cover (above zero, teal) vs dead coral cover (below zero, red) by year. The slow accumulation of dead coral after each bleaching event is clearly visible. Source: AIMS LTMP manta tow data.

The Widening Gap

Recovery Can't Keep Up

Coral science is clear: full recovery from mass bleaching requires 10–15 undisturbed years. The reef is now being hit every 2–3 years. The deficit between recovery needed and recovery received is growing with every event.
Recovery window — available vs required (years between events)

Each bar pair shows available recovery time (teal, actual gap between events) vs required recovery time (red, minimum 10–15 years needed). The deficit — the difference — grows with every successive event. Source: AIMS bleaching records + published coral recovery literature.

Chapter 5b — The Land-Sea Connection

The Reef Doesn't Just Face
Heat — It Faces Poison Too

Every wet season, 35 Queensland river catchments flush sediment, nitrogen and pesticides onto the reef. Inshore reefs — already stressed by bleaching — are simultaneously suffocated by agricultural runoff. The two threats compound each other in ways that make recovery impossible.
Anthropogenic pollutant loads entering the GBR by region (tonnes/yr)

Human-caused (anthropogenic) loads only. Total sediment load reaching the GBR is 17 million tonnes/year — 14 million from human activity. Pesticides are 100% anthropogenic; there is no natural baseline. Source: Reef Water Quality Protection Plan 2009 Baseline Report, GBRMPA.

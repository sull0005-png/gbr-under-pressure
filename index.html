Chapter 1 — The Warming Ocean

The Ocean Is Getting Hotter

Sea surface temperatures above the Great Barrier Reef have been rising for decades. But it is the accelerating frequency of extreme summer heat events that is now overwhelming the reef's ability to recover.

Annual maximum SST anomaly (°C above historical average). Vertical markers indicate confirmed mass bleaching events. Shaded area shows accumulated heat stress above bleaching threshold. Source: NOAA Coral Reef Watch.

Degree heating weeks — accumulated heat stress (1985–2025)

Degree Heating Weeks (DHW) measure cumulative thermal stress. Values above 8°C-weeks trigger severe bleaching and mortality. Source: NOAA Coral Reef Watch.

The bleaching threshold is crossed when temperatures exceed the average summer maximum for 8 consecutive weeks. That threshold is now breached almost every year in at least one sector.
Monthly SST anomaly heatmap — Northern sector

Each row = one year. Each cell = monthly mean SST anomaly. Bleaching events cluster around the hottest austral summers (Jan–Mar).

Chapter 3 — Coral Cover

What's Left of the Reef?

AIMS has tracked hard coral cover across the reef since 1992. After a period of recovery, all three regions suffered dramatic declines following the bleaching events of 2016–2024.

The window is closing

The Reef Has Always Recovered.
Can It Again?

Coral needs 10–15 years between disturbances to rebuild. With bleaching now arriving every 2–3 years and ocean temperatures continuing to rise, scientists warn the recovery window may close entirely within this generation.
